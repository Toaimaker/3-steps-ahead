Deck builder & mana base

The builder opens on the right of your collection. Drag cards in or double-click them, and every change redoes legality, the wildcards you need, the curve, the land drops and the closest meta deck. When the spells are right, Mana base picks the lands.

Open the builder

  • From a deck: press Edit in its window. A deck you cannot change directly (a meta deck, your Arena deck) is copied to the Deck builder group first, and the copy opens.
  • A new deck: the + New deck card at the top of the Deck builder group in Your decks.
  • From the collection: the Deck builder button next to Settings in Collection.

The main window switches to the Collection, filtered on the deck's format, and the builder sits on its right, so you can pick cards from the left and see the deck on the right. Only one builder is open at a time.

Add and remove cards

ActionHow
Add a cardDrag it from the Collection onto the builder (the deck lights up while the card is over it), or double-click it. A card dropped outside the builder does not go in.
Choose where it goesAdd to in the header: Deck, Sideboard or Commander.
Remove a copyClick the card in the deck list.
Add a copyRight-click the card in the deck list.
Find a cardUse the Collection search: name, type or rules text (see Search).
Name and formatType the name in the header; pick the format from its menu. Legality and the card limits follow the format.

Copies are capped at four (one in Brawl), counting deck and sideboard together. Basic lands and cards that say “any number” have no cap.

Cards Arena does not have

For Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage and Pauper the builder accepts cards that are not on Arena, but only through Import: the Collection shows Arena cards.

The data that follows every card

The right side of the builder is laid out like the deck window, and it is redone after each card:

  • Archetype graph, mana curve and lands in the opening hand (the exact chance of 0 to 7 lands in seven cards).
  • Legal or not, with each problem in red: a banned card, a card not in the format, too many copies, too few cards.
  • Cards you own and the wildcards still needed, by rarity (with your collection linked).
  • Land drops on turns 2, 3 and 4 on the play, and sources per colour.
  • The closest meta deck of the format, when the app has its meta, and the copies you share with it.
  • Paper and MTGO prices.

Legality comes from the Standard ban list for Standard and from Scryfall for the other formats. Explorer is checked as Pioneer cards that exist on Arena, because Scryfall has no Explorer list.

Mana base

Build the spells first. Then press Mana base in the Lands section of the list. The lands already in the deck stay, and the ones that are missing are added:

  1. How many lands. Worked out from the meta lists of the same format: the more expensive the spells on average, the more lands, as those lists do. Without lists for the format, 40% of the deck.
  2. How many sources of each colour. Enough to cast each spell on curve most of the time, after Frank Karsten's well-known tables, computed exactly for the size of your deck. A gold card asks for one more source of each of its colours.
  3. Which lands. The best ones first: lands that always enter untapped, then shock, fast, slow and check lands, and tapped lands only when they fix a colour that is short. Four copies of a land (one in Brawl). At equal quality, the lands you own come first.

A MANA BASE box at the top of the data says how many lands were added and why, the sources each colour has against what it needs, and which lands you would have to craft.

Fix mana base and the locks

After the first time the button becomes Fix mana base. Each land in the list has a small lock: lock the lands you want to keep no matter what. Fix mana base removes the unlocked lands and rebuilds the mana around the locked ones, so you can change the spells and fix the lands again without losing your choices. Locks are saved with the deck.

A starting point, not a verdict

The mana base is the best the rules above can find. Your own games may show that you want one land more or less: the deck's Lands in opening hand numbers tell you.

Read the numbers before you play

A new build has no games yet, so the deck window shows what can be known before playing: legality, cards you own, land drops, sources per colour, the closest meta deck, the archetype and the prices. Your own numbers start with your first game.
